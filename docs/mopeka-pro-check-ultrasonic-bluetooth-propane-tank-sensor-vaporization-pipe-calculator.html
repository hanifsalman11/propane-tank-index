Frequently Asked Questions (Mopeka Pro Check Ultrasonic Bluetooth Propane Tank Sensor)

How many gallons of propane per hour does 80,000 BTU burn?

One gallon of liquid propane contains 91,502 BTU of energy. Dividing 80,000 BTU/hr by 91,502 equals 0.87 gallons/hour (3.69 lbs/hr). On 96.0 usable gallons, that lasts 110.3 hours of non-stop 100% burn, or ~13.1 days when cycling 35% of the time.

Why does my 100-lb propane tank freeze up and stop working on a 80,000 BTU heater when it is still 30% full?

Propane liquid must absorb ambient heat through the wetted lower walls of the tank to boil into gas. At 0°F and 20%–30% liquid level, a single 100-lb cylinder can only vaporize ~39,000 BTU/hr. Drawing 80,000 BTU/hr refrigerates the liquid below -44°F (its boiling point), dropping pressure to zero. Manifold two 100-lb tanks together, add a thermostatic tank heater blanket, or upgrade to a 120-gal/250-gal tank.

What size CSST or copper gas line do I need for 80,000 BTU at 45 feet?

Per NFPA 54 low-pressure (11" WC with 0.5" WC pressure drop) tables, delivering 80,000 BTU/hr over 45 feet requires 3/4-Inch CSST Gas Line.
